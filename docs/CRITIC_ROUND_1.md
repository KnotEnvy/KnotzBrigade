# Independent mission critique — round 1

Reviewed 2026-10-17. Scope: the delivered **single three-part mission**, including Hive Whale/Broodkeeper and Bulb Whale/Furnace Warden. This is a playable, coherent mission with a useful defensive hook and attractive art direction. It does **not** yet support scores above 8.8 in every category. None of the ten categories currently clears that threshold.

## Evidence and limits

- Inspected `remake/src/core/mission.ts`, `MissionScene.ts`, `OceanScene.ts`, `HarborScene.ts`, `PilotInput.ts`, `Feedback.ts`, `AudioBus.ts`, `main.ts`, `style.css`, `index.html`, and the mission test script/results.
- Independently tried T3 `preview_open`; it failed because its host lacks the documented browser libraries. Used the provided Playwright/Chromium fallback against the already-running `http://127.0.0.1:5197/`.
- Exercised the actual live browser: normal launch, default auto-fire, idle survival, directional keyboard movement, guard, pause/resume, return to harbor, Auto-fire focus behavior, touch-emulated portrait launch/guard/pause/resume, and landscape launch/layout. The fresh live Pilot/Lance idle run failed at **36.605 mission seconds**, with 9 kills, 3,020 score, 8 damage including repairs, and no abilities used. The starting idle position is therefore not a safe-win exploit.
- Used **controlled boss observation fixtures**: started a checkpoint and advanced the wave clock to spawn each boss, with auto-fire off. These were pattern/readability observations, not ordinary mission clears. Health/damage were not boosted to obtain a win. Errors from my inspection helper reading a destroyed group or serializing a circular object were helper errors, not game crashes.
- Read the existing input-only deterministic full-run results. Those establish reachability and expose timing differences; they are not human playtests. Their large part banners can be artifacts of accelerated scene updates versus wall-clock DOM timers. Normal live banners did dismiss; I do not count those simulated banners as a gameplay bug.
- `npm run check` passed, including the Phaser 4.2.1 assertion and TypeScript check. My live browser recorded no uncaught page errors. I did not rerun/overwrite the full mission test evidence.
- Software WebGL rendering on this shared host is not representative device performance. I cannot certify “buttery smooth” from it. I could inspect sound state/wiring but could not listen to the live mix, so the audio score is provisional. No physical gamepad, physical phone, or human enjoyment study was performed.

## Fixed scorecard

Eight means good with clear rough edges; nine means polished, compelling execution. Scores assess this mission's mechanics/execution, not campaign length or production budget.

| Category | /10 | Reason and current blocker |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Moment-to-moment fun | **7.5** | Immediate shooting, small vulnerable core, pickups, guard/counter and Surge provide a sound action loop. However, many waves resolve through the same right-to-left alignment task, and Lance can erase bosses before their defining patterns develop. The loop has promise; sustained enjoyment is not established by bot clears. |
| 2. Controls/game feel | **8.0** | Normalized diagonals, immediate movement, focus speed, automatic fire and real pause are sensible. Keyboard steering and guard worked. Auto-fire checkbox focus disables P/Escape shortcuts, and touch lacks the precision-focus action. These are meaningful polish gaps. |
| 3. Encounter pacing/three-part structure | **7.0** | Three named parts, checkpoints, palette changes and the engine-room reveal form a complete arc. The encounter grammar changes less than the scenery: most of the same enemy jobs recur, `Wave.pattern` is stored but never used, and two thermal-vent events carry most of part three's environmental distinction. Boss duration varies sharply with loadout. |
| 4. Enemy variety/fairness | **7.4** | Aimed spread, delayed dive, radial fire, fast wobbling enemies and optional courier targets offer readable roles. Red-lane warnings and hit invulnerability are fair-minded. Drone and stalker movement overlap in purpose; cross/sine authoring flags do not create distinct behavior. The “pearl mines” lesson describes destructible mines that are not implemented. |
| 5. Miniboss/final guardian design | **6.5** | Large animated silhouettes, health bars, an overload threshold, reinforcements and lane warnings are a foundation. Both bosses run essentially the same aimed-volley → semicircle fan → horizontal lane cycle. The final adds bullets/twin lanes, rather than a substantially different tactical problem. Optimized Lance kills shorten both fights enough to undercut their phases. |
| 6. Visual identity/readability | **7.6** | Brass machinery, aquatic monsters, cyan shots and the foundry destination create a recognizable setting. The harbor and engine-room image are strong. Foreground machinery sits above ships/enemies, the mobile core is tiny, and additive white damage tint repeatedly removes the bosses' attractive detail. Decoration sometimes competes with the action. |
| 7. Audio/feedback | **7.0 provisional** | Hit bursts, recoil tilt, score floaters, shield/counter cues, pooled sound voices, mute/volume and reduced-motion support are present. Distinct encounter/phase audio and listening validation remain needed. |
| 8. Engagement/replay | **7.1** | Two rigs, three flights, chains, couriers, local records and next-run prompts give reasons to return. Rank thresholds compress very different performances into S+: existing clean clears score roughly 50k–107k against a 26k threshold. Records combine the differently balanced rigs. Unbounded scoring from repeat boss reinforcements/catches makes score competition vulnerable to stalling. |
| 9. Reliability/performance | **8.0** | Type checking passes; prior tests cover core collisions, guard, invulnerability, pause and full mission completion; my live checks had no uncaught game errors. Pause UI state has reproducible flaws. Real-device frame pacing, input latency and longer-session behavior are not established by the accelerated tests or software renderer. |

## Prioritized blockers and smallest high-impact changes

1. **Give the two bosses different jobs and preserve their signature actions under burst damage.** Existing Pilot/Lance results encounter the miniboss at 79.55s and leave it at 87.82s: about **8.27s**. The guardian lasts about **10.40s**. Pilot/Fan takes about **13.97s** and **34.48s** respectively. These are bot timings, not claims that every player finds them easy. Nevertheless, the miniboss's first lane warning normally comes only near its third attack, so a strong rig can defeat it before that action matters. Increasing HP alone would make Fan's long fight worse. Tune effective rig damage and encounter flow together, then verify both phases' important actions actually execute against maximum attainable burst.

   Make the **Broodkeeper** the guard/counter lesson: a conspicuous catchable volley, a brood deployment that changes the firing lane, and a brief, clearly exposed core that rewards a returned shot. Make the **Furnace Warden** the foundry's spatial test: telegraphed vents with a moving safe gap, alternating exposed machinery/core targets, and an overload that combines those established rules. It should ask the player to read the room, rather than repeat the carrier's fan with more bullets. A single well-executed signature interaction per boss is worth more than several extra generic attacks. Avoid turning this into lengthy mandatory invulnerability.

2. **Make each part mechanically identifiable.** Implement the existing formation-pattern field or replace it with actual authored paths; currently `enemy.pattern` and `baseY` do not drive movement. Have part one teach aim/guard, part two use brood/escort formations, and part three combine a recognizable vent sequence with the learned enemy roles. Rework a few existing waves into setup → payoff → recovery combinations before adding more enemy art or extending the mission. Replace “Pearl mines · destroy them before they bloom” with an accurate radial-fire instruction unless destructible mines are implemented.

3. **Fix the verified input/layout defects.** After toggling Auto-fire, P/Escape are ignored because `main.ts` skips all `INPUT` targets. Restore playfield focus or narrow that exclusion. Reset the Pause label on harbor/new-run transitions: pausing then leaving can retain “Resume · P” during a new active run. Keep a visible pause control on touch landscape. Add a touch Focus action or equivalent precise steering. At 844×390 the launch button extends from y353.97 to y394.97 while its clipped playfield ends at y381.98; use a compact landscape harbor layout and hide flight controls until a mission begins. Increase essential mobile HUD text instead of shrinking every desktop field.

4. **Protect action visibility.** `OceanScene` puts the foreground at depth 35, above the player's 25 and normal enemies' 22. Fade/reposition that layer where it crosses combatants, or draw combatants above it. Preserve boss texture during ordinary hits; the current white additive tint makes sustained hits resemble an untextured white cutout. Give the vulnerable core a readable minimum screen size on phones and keep hit flashes, warning lanes and hostile bullets distinct from pickups/decoration. Correct the default “hold Space to fire” lesson when auto-fire is already on; say “destroy the courier, then collect its relay” to avoid implying that touching an enemy is the pickup action.

5. **Close score-farming paths, then calibrate rank/replay goals.** This is a source-derived exploit, not a completed long farm: bosses have no timeout, the carrier repeatedly spawns score-bearing drones, and absorption/grazes continue awarding points. A player can delay the kill indefinitely. Bound rewarded reinforcements and repeat defensive scoring per encounter, or otherwise ensure stalling cannot dominate the record. Calibrate rank bands from varied legitimate runs after rig balance is fixed. Separate rig records or show their differences clearly. A modest, visible mastery goal for each part would add more replay value than another generic score counter.

6. **Validate sound and frame pacing on the delivery targets.** Retain the useful feedback pool and reduced-motion option. Add distinct warning/phase cues and restrained part/boss music changes, then listen for cue clarity under sustained fire. Check an ordinary full mission on representative desktop and touch hardware with render-frame timing; accelerated scene updates cannot certify it. Selectable render quality may help weaker devices, but my software-renderer observations do not prove a particular device's performance.

## Comparison standard

The useful comparison with R-Type/Gradius is the quality of the decisions inside a stage, not the number of stages. R-Type's official description emphasizes mastering the Wave Cannon and Force; the original Gradius manual describes collecting capsules and choosing when to activate a power boost. Those mechanics attach consequences to positioning and timing. Tideguard can give this mission its own comparable decision-making role, but current damage balance often lets ordinary shooting finish the job first. See the [official R-Type description](https://rtypefinal2.com/?lang=en) and [Nintendo-hosted Gradius manual](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/clv/manuals/en/pdf/CLV-P-NABRE_en.pdf).

My judgment is that this mission has a stronger presentation and a more usable shell than its current encounter depth. It should keep its aquatic foundry identity and forgiving retry structure. To earn nine-level scores, its guard mechanic, formations, boss phases, scoring and device presentation need to reinforce one another in ordinary play. This report does not claim that the mission objectively beats either series, and it does not claim human-tested addictiveness.
