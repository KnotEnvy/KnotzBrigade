# Independent mission critique — round 4

Reviewed 2026-10-17. Scope: **the single three-part mission**, including the Broodkeeper miniboss and Furnace Warden. This is a strong, complete horizontal shooter mission with a distinctive defensive mechanic, attractive underwater foundry identity, and meaningful practice goals. My fixed scorecard is **8.83/10 mean**, with **eight categories strictly above 8.8**. Audio remains **8.3 provisional** and reliability/performance **8.8**. The requested **every-category-above-8.8 outcome remains unmet**. Neither campaign length nor production budget is a deduction; the threshold has not been relaxed to manufacture a pass.

## Remaining objections

**No new confirmed gameplay defect was found in this round.** The prior practice-flow bug is independently fixed. The revised courier pressure and ordinary-enemy contrast address the specific round-3 design/readability objections observed here. I would not add more enemies, extend the mission, or change its core mechanics merely to force these decimals upward.

Further improvement is now blocked chiefly by **missing listening, representative hardware, and human feedback**. Listen through sustained fire, Guard/counter, overlapping warnings, a core opening and overload; assess cue clarity and whether the single-loop finale treatment works. Play a complete normally rendered flight on a representative desktop and physical phone, checking frame pacing through both bosses and simultaneous steering/Focus/Guard. Observe a first-time player's understanding of catching, armor openings and optional relays, then whether the debrief creates a concrete reason to retry. Those observations should determine the next changes. Another source-only critic round cannot substitute for them.

The scores do not allege a broken mix or slow physical device. They state where the available evidence stops. Likewise, a bot clear cannot establish genuine human enjoyment or objective addictiveness.

## R-Type / Gradius comparison

The useful standard is how a single stage makes positioning, commitment and recovery matter. R-Type's official description centers its Wave Cannon and Force; the original Gradius manual describes collecting capsules and choosing when to activate an available power boost. See the [official R-Type description](https://rtypefinal2.com/?lang=en) and [Nintendo-hosted Gradius manual](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/clv/manuals/en/pdf/CLV-P-NABRE_en.pdf).

My editorial comparison is that this mission now has its own credible answer: Guard turns incoming fire into an alignment opportunity, Surge trades immediate safety against an opening, gates offer both passage constraints and cover, and optional relays ask for a detour. The two bosses test different applications of those rules. Damage preserves upgrades and direct practice shortens recovery, giving this stage a forgiving rhythm. This is convincing execution of a complete first mission; this review does **not** establish that it matches either series' best stage craft, beats them overall, or has human-tested replay appeal.
