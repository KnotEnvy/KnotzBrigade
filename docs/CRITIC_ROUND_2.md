# Independent mission critique — round 2

Reviewed 2026-10-17. Scope: the **single three-part mission**, including the Broodkeeper miniboss and Furnace Warden. This revision is substantially better than round 1: the defensive mechanic now has a purpose in the bosses, formations and mines actually exist, rig clear times are comparable, and the earlier input/layout defects are repaired. My current mean is **8.35/10**. **No category yet exceeds 8.8.** These are candid editorial judgments, not a measurement of human enjoyment or a gate adjusted to produce a passing result. Campaign length is not a deduction.

## Fixed scorecard

Eight means good with visible rough edges; nine means polished and compelling execution. The reasons below distinguish observed/source-derived reservations from validation limits.

Ordinary wave play still spends much of its time aligning with successive enemies arriving from the right; stronger authored combinations would give more memorable decisions between the bosses. This is a design judgment supported by live samples and the wave schedule, not a claim of tested human enjoyment. |

Controls/game feel | **8.7** | Immediate normalized movement, a useful Focus speed, auto-fire, forgiving core, working keyboard shortcuts, separate touch Focus and real pause form a strong control set. ## Highest-impact feasible next changes

1. **Make the new boss rules visibly self-explanatory.** Give armor, hatch/core opening and overload a persistent, conspicuous actor treatment: a changed hatch/plate, a bright exposed component or restrained aura, and a recognizably different armored-hit response. Keep ordinary boss texture visible. The existing core rings/HUD can reinforce that signal. When the links break, say “Surge now” only if Surge is ready; otherwise give an accurate attack-window instruction. The captured core-opening fixture shows **39% charge** beside the unconditional prompt. This is a concrete UI defect, even though the fixture itself is synthetic.

2. **Re-author a few wave combinations into setup → decision → recovery.** Do not extend the stage merely to add more enemies. For part two, have a clearly announced mine layer establish a lane while its crossing escort creates a deliberate alternative route, then leave space to collect its reward. For part three, develop the existing vents into a short alternating sequence with a readable safe route; place an optional courier so pursuing it trades safety for reward, then provide recovery. Use the existing enemy roles and tells. This would connect the parts' ordinary waves to their bosses and make the three-part identity mechanical as well as scenic. These are design recommendations, not bug fixes or a guarantee of a particular score.

3. **Give replay a specific learning target.** Record a concise per-part summary of damage, relay, catches and best chain, and show one attainable improvement on the debrief/checkpoint screen. A practice-part personal best or clean-part indicator would make checkpoint retries useful for mastery rather than only reaching the ending. The current full-run S+ requirement is already meaningful; preserve its integrity and bounded scoring. Avoid adding unrelated unlock systems before this feedback is clear.

4. **Simplify the phone HUD around decisions.** Keep hull, Guard readiness, Surge percentage and boss state readable at native size; reduce or move decorative/secondary fields. Enlarge the relevant boss-state instruction instead of retaining all desktop labels. The repaired outside-arena controls and compact landscape launch should be preserved. Validate two-thumb movement/Focus/Guard combinations on an actual phone before claiming polished touch feel.

5. **Close the validation gaps honestly.** Listen to sustained-fire, Guard, overload and simultaneous-warning passages and adjust cue priority/music contrast from that evidence. Measure an ordinary fully rendered mission on a representative GPU and phone at each quality level. These are evidence requirements for confident audio/performance conclusions, not proof that the current game is slow or sounds bad. Human first-play/replay observation would also show whether the new decisions create the intended enjoyment; bot clean clears cannot answer that.

The mission is now a coherent, appealing shooter with its own defensive hook and two distinct confrontations. The remaining work is chiefly encounter composition, state communication and validation, rather than another broad feature expansion. This round does not establish the requested every-category-above-8.8 outcome.
